Taiyuan

A thousand years of history, one majestic city

Top Attractions

Jinci Temple

Location: 25 km southwest of Taiyuan city center, Jinci District

History: Founded in the Northern Wei dynasty (386-534 AD), Jinci is China's oldest ancestral temple, dedicated to King Shuyu of Tang, and expanded over 1,500 years by successive dynasties.

Highlights:

  • The Nanlao Spring, a natural spring with crystal-clear water that never freezes
  • The exquisite Song dynasty (11th century) female statues in the Sage Mother Hall
  • The 3,000-year-old Zhou cypress tree, a living relic of ancient China

Hours: 8:00 AM - 6:00 PM (April-October); 8:30 AM - 5:30 PM (November-March)

Tickets: 80 RMB (peak season), 60 RMB (off-peak); free for children under 1.2m

Transport: Bus 308, 804, or 856 from Taiyuan Railway Station; taxi about 40 minutes from city center

Duration: 3-4 hours

Best season: Spring (April-May) and autumn (September-October) for mild weather and lush scenery

Tips: Arrive early to avoid tour groups; combine with nearby Tianlongshan Grottoes for a full day trip.

Twin Pagodas Temple (Yongzuo Temple)

Location: Hedong Road, Yingze District, Taiyuan city center

History: Built in 1608 during the Ming dynasty, the Twin Pagodas are the symbol of Taiyuan. The East Pagoda is 54.76m tall, the West 54.78m, making them the tallest twin pagodas in China.

Highlights:

  • Climb the narrow spiral staircases to the top for panoramic city views
  • The intricate brick-carved Buddhist reliefs on each pagoda level
  • The tranquil temple courtyards with ancient pines and peonies in spring

Hours: 8:00 AM - 5:30 PM (daily)

Tickets: 30 RMB (includes both pagodas and temple grounds)

Transport: Bus 21, 606, or 825 to Shuangta Si stop; metro line 1 (under construction, future stop nearby)

Duration: 2-3 hours

Best season: Year-round, but spring (April) for blooming peonies and clear skies

Tips: Climbing the pagodas is physically demanding – narrow and steep, wear comfortable shoes; photography best in the late afternoon light.

Shanxi Museum

Location: No. 13 Binhe West Road, Wanbailin District, near the Fen River

History: Opened in 2004, this is one of China's largest provincial museums, housing over 400,000 artifacts that chronicle Shanxi's 3,000-year civilization, from Bronze Age dynasties to the Ming-Qing era.

Highlights:

  • The bronze collection from the Shang and Zhou dynasties, including ritual vessels with intricate taotie motifs
  • The Jin dynasty (1115-1234) tomb murals and painted sculptures, rarely seen elsewhere
  • The 'Dragon' exhibition hall with imperial artifacts from the Ming and Qing courts

Hours: 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM (closed Mondays; last entry 4:00 PM)

Tickets: Free (passport required for entry; advance reservation online recommended)

Transport: Bus 865, 813, or 851 to Shanbo Museum stop; taxi from city center about 15 minutes

Duration: 3-4 hours (at least 2 hours for full highlights)

Best season: Year-round indoors; avoid Chinese national holidays (Oct 1-7, May 1-3) for smaller crowds

Tips: Download the free audio guide app (QR code at entrance); check special exhibitions on the official WeChat account.

Mengshan Giant Buddha

Location: Mengshan Mountain, 20 km southwest of Taiyuan, Jinyuan District

History: Carved in 551 AD during the Northern Qi dynasty, this 63-meter tall seated Buddha is 200 years older than the Leshan Giant Buddha. It was lost for centuries and rediscovered in 1980, with restoration completed in 2008.

Highlights:

  • The sheer scale – the head alone is over 8 meters tall, and the stone body merges with the mountain
  • The modern open-air staircase leading to the Buddha's feet for close-up views
  • Sunset views over the Fen River valley from the viewing platform

Hours: 8:30 AM - 5:30 PM (April-October); 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM (November-March)

Tickets: 50 RMB (includes mountain shuttle bus)

Transport: Bus 58 from Taiyuan city center to Mengshan stop; taxi about 35 minutes from downtown

Duration: 2-3 hours (including shuttle and walking)

Best season: Spring and autumn are ideal; summer is hot and exposed on the mountain

Tips: Wear hiking shoes – the final section is a steep climb; bring water, few vendors on the mountain; combine with nearby Jinci for a full day.

Local Cuisine

class="food-item" style="margin-bottom: 3rem;">

Taiyuan Noodle (Tiaohu)

Taste: Chewy, slippery, and hearty, with a slightly nutty flavor from high-gluten wheat flour; served in a rich lamb or pork broth.

Story: Invented during the Tang dynasty, Tiaohu was a staple for miners and laborers in Shanxi's coal country. Legend says a local chef accidentally dropped dough into boiling water, and the resulting strips were so loved that they became a regional icon.

How to eat: Eat it hot with a spoonful of chili oil and Shanxi mature vinegar; pair with a side of pickled cucumber or garlic soy dip.

Price: 15-25 RMB per bowl

Shanxi Mature Vinegar (Lao Chen Cu)

Taste: Deep, smoky, and tangy with a complex umami; aged for at least 2 years, it has a mellow sweetness under the sharp acidity.

Story: Shanxi vinegar has been brewed for over 3,000 years, with the 'Laochen' style perfected in Taiyuan during the Qing dynasty. It was served to Empress Dowager Cixi, who reportedly praised it above all other vinegars in China.

How to eat: Use it as a dipping sauce for dumplings and noodles, or mix a few drops into hot soup; also great as a salad dressing with sesame oil.

Price: 20-50 RMB per bottle (local brands like Ninghuafu or Zijiang)

Jin-style Braised Pork Ribs (Pai Gu)

Taste: Tender, fall-off-the-bone pork with a sweet-savory glaze of rock sugar, star anise, and Shaoxing wine; rich and slightly oily with a sticky finish.

Story: Originating from the Ming dynasty's Shanxi merchants, this dish was a celebration staple for prosperous trade families. The slow-braising method reflects the region's coal-fired clay ovens, which gave ribs a unique smoky tenderness.

How to eat: Eat with steamed buns (mantou) to soak up the sauce; often served as part of a multi-course feast at local restaurants.

Price: 45-70 RMB per plate (serves 2-3)

Culture & Traditions

Shanxi Opera (Jinju)

History: Jinju, or Shanxi Opera, formed in the mid-Ming dynasty (14th century) from local folk tunes and evolved under the patronage of Shanxi merchants. It features stilt-walking, face-changing, and a distinctive high-pitched vocal style.

Experience: Performances include traditional operatic singing, acrobatic combat, and elaborate costumes; sessions often start with a short recital explaining the plot.

Venues: Taiyuan Shanxi Opera Troupe Theater (No. 88 Xinghualing Street); also at the Shanxi Grand Theatre on weekends

Best time: Friday and Saturday evenings at 7:30 PM; inquire at the venue for seasonal schedules

Significance: Jinju is a living heritage of Shanxi mercantile culture, blending Confucian virtues, local legends, and the resilience of northern Chinese communities.

Guandi Temple Fair (Guandi Miao Hui)

History: Dating back to the Ming dynasty, this fair celebrates Lord Guan (Guandi), the god of loyalty and wealth, brought to Taiyuan by Shanxi merchants. It was revived in 2006 and now features parades, folk rituals, and traditional crafts.

Experience: Visitors can watch religious processions with dragon and lion dances, try DIY sugar-figure painting, and attend morning chanting at the temple; local stalls sell clay figurines and embroidered shoes.

Venues: Taiyuan Guandi Temple, No. 42 Miaoguan Street, Yingze District

Best time: Annual fair on the 13th day of the fifth lunar month (usually June/July), 9 AM - 5 PM

Significance: The fair embodies Shanxi's merchant spirit of integrity and community solidarity, while preserving centuries-old artisan crafts and ritual performances.

Travel Guide

4.1 Best time to visit

Spring (April-May)： Mild temperatures (10-22°C), blooming peonies at Twin Pagodas, and clear skies ideal for outdoor temple visits. Rain is occasional, so pack a light jacket.

Autumn (September-October)： Crisp air, golden leaves on the mountains, and comfortable 15-25°C. Perfect for hiking Mengshan and enjoying outdoor markets. Best season for photography.

Avoid (July-August and December-February)： Summer peaks at 35°C with high humidity, making sightseeing exhausting. Winter can drop to -10°C, and some mountain sites may close due to snow. If visiting in winter, stick to museums and indoor food tours.

4.2 Recommended days

Short (1-2 days)： Day 1: Twin Pagodas + Shanxi Museum; Day 2: Jinci Temple (half-day) and try Taiyuan noodles for lunch. Return the same afternoon.

Ideal (3-5 days)： Add Mengshan Giant Buddha and a Jinju opera evening. Spend a day exploring the old city alleys (Liu Xiang and Zhonglou Street) and vinegar factory workshops. Take a side trip to Pingyao Ancient City (1.5h by train).

Deep (7+ days)： Include Wutai Mountain (2-day trip), Yungang Grottoes in Datong (1 day by high-speed rail), and the Hukou Waterfall (2-day tour). In Taiyuan, dedicate a full day to the Shanxi Museum's special exhibitions and a cooking class for Shanxi vinegar dishes.

4.3 Getting around

Metro/Bus： Metro Line 1 runs from eastern to western outskirts (opened 2021); buses (2 RMB) cover all major sites. Use the 'Taiyuan Tong' app for routes and payments; buses can be slow at peak hours.

Taxi/Ride-hailing： Didi and local taxis are plentiful and affordable (start 8 RMB). A ride from city center to Jinci costs about 120 RMB. Avoid hailing taxis around 5-7 PM when shifts change.

Shared bikes： Meituan and Hello scooters are widely available near the Fen River and city parks. Ideal for short hops between Twin Pagodas and the museum, but not for hilly areas like Mengshan.

4.4 Airport & intercity

Taiyuan Wusu International Airport (TYN)： Located 15 km south of downtown. Airport bus line 1 (10 RMB) runs to Yingze Street every 30 min; taxi costs 40-60 RMB and takes about 25 min.

High-speed rail： Taiyuan South Railway Station connects to Beijing (2.5h), Xi'an (3h), and Datong (1.5h). Metro Line 1 connects the station to the city center.

Going to other cities： From Taiyuan South, Pingyao (30 min), Wutai Mountain (2h), and Luoyang (4h). Long-distance buses from Taiyuan Coach Station serve nearby towns like Qiao's Family Compound.

4.5 Practical tips

  • Carry your passport or ID at all times – required for free museum entry and some temple ticket counters.
  • Learn two phrases: 'Jiá miàn' (noodles with extra toppings) and 'Bù là' (not spicy) – your meals will be more pleasant.
  • The local vinegar is a great souvenir, but buy from branded shops (Ninghuafu) to avoid cheap imitation bottles.
  • Beware of 'black taxis' near railway stations – always use the app or the official taxi line to avoid overcharging.
  • Wear non-slip shoes at Jinci and Mengshan – stone stairways are often damp with moss even in dry weather.
