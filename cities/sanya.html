Sanya

Where Tropical Paradise Meets the South China Sea

Core Scenic Spots

Yalong Bay (Ya Long Wan)

Location: About 25 km southeast of central Sanya, on the city's eastern coast

Historical Background: Yalong Bay is the most celebrated beach in Sanya and is often called the "Oriental Hawaii" — a sweeping 7.5-kilometer crescent of powder-white sand curving around a calm, turquoise bay. The name "Yalong" (Dragon Subdued) comes from local legend: the bay is said to have been formed when a heavenly dragon descended to the sea, leaving behind the crescent shoreline and a cluster of offshore islets. For centuries this stretch of coast was a quiet fishing shore, its remoteness keeping it almost untouched. It was not until the 1990s, as Hainan developed into an international tropical resort destination, that Yalong Bay was transformed into the island's flagship beach — now lined with luxury hotels, yet still prized for having the clearest, most swimmable water in Sanya. The bay's sand is famously fine and white, and the water so transparent that the seabed is visible far from shore.

Highlights:

  • The Crescent Beach — 7.5 km of fine white sand, ideal for swimming, sunbathing, and long walks
  • Water Sports — jet skiing, parasailing, banana boats, and glass-bottom boat rides over the coral
  • Yalong Bay Tropical Paradise Forest Park — the lush hillside behind the bay, with walking trails, viewing platforms, and panoramic sea views
  • The Offshore Islets — small, green islands dotting the bay that add to the postcard scenery
  • Beachfront Resorts & Dining — a string of hotels and open-air seafood restaurants along the shore

Opening Hours: The public beach is open all day; water-sports and park areas generally 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM

Tickets: The main beach is free; Yalong Bay Tropical Paradise Forest Park is about ¥150 (with shuttle)

Transportation: About 30–40 minutes by taxi (¥60–80) from downtown Sanya; public buses 15, 25, and 27 run to Yalong Bay; many hotels offer shuttle services

Best Duration: Half a day to a full day — time for swimming, water sports, and the forest park

Best Season: November to April, when the weather is dry, sunny, and mild (24–28°C); the water is clearest in these months

Tips: Bring sunscreen, a hat, and swimwear — the tropical sun is intense even in "winter." Morning is the calmest and clearest time for swimming. Most beach chairs and umbrellas are operated by the hotels, so check before using them. Combine the beach with the forest park's viewing platform for the classic panoramic photo of the bay.

Tianya Haijiao (Tian Ya Hai Jiao)

Location: On the southwestern tip of Sanya, about 20 km from the city center

Historical Background: Tianya Haijiao — literally "the Edge of the Sky and the End of the Sea" — is Sanya's most iconic and romantic landmark. It is a rugged stretch of coastline scattered with enormous granite boulders, some of which bear ancient inscriptions. The most famous are two great rocks carved with the four characters "Tianya" (Sky's Edge) and "Haijiao" (Sea's Corner). The "Tianya" inscription was carved in 1733 by the Qing-dynasty official Cheng Zhe, and "Haijiao" was added later; a third stone bears the characters "Nantian Yizhu" ("A Single Pillar of the Southern Sky"). In ancient times this far southern coast was the remote edge of the empire, a place of exile and longing — poets imagined it as the literal end of the world. Over the centuries that melancholy romance was transformed, and today Tianya Haijiao is celebrated as a place of enduring love: couples come to pledge that their bond will last "to the ends of the earth," and the site is a beloved backdrop for wedding photography.

Highlights:

  • The "Tianya" and "Haijiao" Inscribed Rocks — the two legendary boulders that define the site
  • "Nantian Yizhu" Stone — a third inscribed rock, meaning "A Single Pillar of the Southern Sky"
  • The Rocky Coastline — granite boulders, crashing surf, and dramatic sea views
  • The Lovers' Road & Wedding Plaza — pathways and a plaza designed for couples and wedding photos
  • Historical & Cultural Exhibits — displays on the site's literary and exile history

Opening Hours: 7:30 AM – 6:00 PM daily

Tickets: About ¥81 (entry); an electric cart within the site is an optional extra (about ¥15)

Transportation: About 30 minutes by taxi (¥50–60) from downtown; buses 16, 21, 24, 25, and 29 stop near the scenic area

Best Duration: 2–3 hours for the rocks, coastline walk, and photo stops

Best Season: November to April; late afternoon offers the most beautiful light and cooler temperatures

Tips: Arrive early or late to avoid the midday heat and crowds — the site has little shade. The rocks can be slippery when wet, so wear sturdy footwear. If you want the classic photo in front of the "Tianya" and "Haijiao" boulders, expect a queue during peak hours. The coastal views and sunset are the real highlights, so allow time to simply wander the shore.

Wuzhizhou Island (Wu Zhi Zhou Dao)

Location: In Haitang Bay, about 30 km northeast of central Sanya; reached by a short ferry

Historical Background: Wuzhizhou Island is a small, butterfly-shaped island in Haitang Bay, celebrated as the best place in Hainan for diving and water sports. Its name means "lovebird island," and legend holds that two devoted lovers were transformed into the island's twin peaks. For most of its history the island was a quiet, forested outpost, at times used as a military lookout; its surrounding waters, rich in coral and marine life, were known mainly to local fishermen. With Sanya's rise as a resort city, Wuzhizhou was developed into a world-class marine tourism destination, though its ecology is carefully protected. The island is ringed by some of the clearest water in the region, with visibility often reaching 15–25 meters, and its fringing coral reefs shelter colorful tropical fish, sea turtles, and a dazzling array of marine life. It is now regarded as one of China's premier diving spots.

Highlights:

  • Scuba Diving & Snorkeling — the island's crown jewel, with clear water and vibrant coral reefs
  • Water Sports — jet skiing, parasailing, sea-walking, and banana boats
  • The Coral Viewing Platform & Underwater Walk — ways to see marine life without full diving gear
  • Beach & Swimming Areas — white-sand coves and designated safe swimming zones
  • The Lover's Bridge & Viewpoints — scenic spots overlooking the turquoise bay

Opening Hours: Ferries generally run 8:00 AM – 4:00 PM (last return ferry around 5:30 PM); confirm times in advance

Tickets: About ¥144 for the round-trip ferry plus island entry; diving and water sports are priced separately

Transportation: Taxi from downtown Sanya about 40–50 minutes (¥100–120) to the Wuzhizhou Wharf; public bus 28 runs to Haitang Bay; from the wharf, a 20-minute ferry reaches the island

Best Duration: A full day, especially if you plan to dive or snorkel

Best Season: October to April, when the water is calmest and visibility highest; avoid typhoon season (roughly July to September)

Tips: Book diving and snorkeling in advance, especially in peak season. Arrive early to catch the first ferry and beat the crowds. Bring swimwear, sunscreen, and a towel; lockers are available. If you are prone to seasickness, sit in the rear of the ferry. Respect the marine environment — do not touch or step on the coral.

Nanshan Cultural Tourism Zone (Nan Shan)

Location: On the southwest coast of Sanya, about 40 km west of the city center

Historical Background: The Nanshan Cultural Tourism Zone is a vast seaside complex that blends Buddhist culture, Taoist tradition, and tropical scenery, built around one of the most striking sights in Hainan: a 108-meter-tall bronze statue of Guanyin, the Bodhisattva of Compassion, standing on a platform in the sea. The statue — one of the tallest of its kind in the world — was completed in 2005 and depicts Guanyin with three faces, each looking out over a different direction, embodying her compassion toward all beings. The zone also preserves the tradition of "Nanshan," a mountain associated in Chinese culture with longevity and the phrase "as enduring as Nanshan." Within the grounds are a major Buddhist temple, the Nanshan Temple, with its own towering Guanyin hall, as well as gardens, pavilions, and a beautiful beach. The site is both a working place of worship and one of Hainan's most important cultural landmarks.

Highlights:

  • The 108-Meter Guanyin Statue — the three-faced bronze colossus standing in the sea, reached by a causeway
  • Nanshan Temple — a grand Buddhist temple with halls, bell and drum towers, and a golden Guanyin hall
  • The Longevity Corridor & Gardens — landscaped paths, pavilions, and bamboo groves dedicated to the theme of longevity
  • The Beach & Guanyin Platform — a shoreline walk offering sea views of the statue
  • Buddhist Rituals & Vegetarian Dining — opportunities to observe ceremonies and sample temple vegetarian cuisine

Opening Hours: 8:00 AM – 5:30 PM daily

Tickets: About ¥108 for the full zone (an electric cart within the site is an optional extra)

Transportation: About 50 minutes by taxi (¥120–150) from downtown; tourist bus routes 16 and 25 connect to the Nanshan area

Best Duration: Half a day to a full day — the grounds are large and best explored at a relaxed pace

Best Season: November to April; mornings are cooler and less crowded, and the sea light on the statue is most beautiful

Tips: Dress modestly when entering the temple and statue areas, and be respectful of worshippers. The site is large, so wear comfortable shoes and consider the electric cart to save energy. Bring water and sun protection, as much of the walking is outdoors. Combine with a visit to the nearby beach and allow time to walk the causeway out to the Guanyin statue for close-up views.

Featured Cuisine

Wenchang Chicken (文昌鸡)

Flavor Profile: Hainan's most famous dish and the first of the island's "Four Famous Dishes" — a free-range chicken gently poached whole until just cooked, then chilled to firm the skin, leaving the meat tender, juicy, and clean-tasting with a thin layer of golden fat beneath the glossy skin. The flavor is delicate and natural, but the dish comes alive through its dipping sauce: a vivid, aromatic blend of minced ginger, chopped garlic, spring onion, salt, fresh lime juice, and a splash of the poaching broth, sometimes with a little chili. The sharp, fragrant sauce lifts the chicken's subtle sweetness, and the whole experience is one of pristine, unfussy flavor — the essence of Hainanese cooking.

Historical & Cultural Story: Wenchang Chicken takes its name from Wenchang, a town on Hainan's northeast coast where the birds are traditionally raised free-range on a diet of rice bran, coconut, and peanut — a regimen long credited with producing the island's most tender, flavorful chicken. It is a dish of deep cultural importance, served at festivals, banquets, wedding feasts, and family reunions across Hainan, and it traveled with Hainanese emigrants to Southeast Asia, where it evolved into the world-famous Hainanese chicken rice. For Hainanese families, the ability to poach a chicken to the perfect point of tenderness is a point of pride, and the dish is the standard by which a cook's skill is often judged.

How to Eat:

  1. Take a piece of chicken and dip it generously into the ginger-lime sauce
  2. Eat it with plain rice, ideally cooked with a little chicken fat or broth
  3. Enjoy a spoonful of the poaching broth alongside
  4. Appreciate the texture contrast of tender meat and springy skin

Price Range: ¥60–120 for a whole chicken at a restaurant; ¥25–50 for a portion

Where to Try:

  • Hainanese restaurants across Sanya — order it as the centerpiece of a meal
  • Seafood and family restaurants near the bays — many serve it as a signature dish
  • Local "chicken rice" eateries — for the classic pairing with fragrant rice

Hele Crab (和乐蟹)

Flavor Profile: Hainan's most celebrated seafood and another of the island's "Four Famous Dishes" — a blue-green swimming crab prized for the exceptional sweetness of its white meat and the rich, buttery depth of its golden roe. The crab is almost always simply steamed, a method that preserves its delicate, briny flavor, sometimes with a scattering of ginger, spring onion, and a splash of rice wine. The result is tender, sweet flesh in the claws and legs and, in season, generous roe in the shell, all best enjoyed with a light ginger-vinegar or lime dipping sauce. It is seafood at its purest, needing almost nothing to shine.

Historical & Cultural Story: Hele Crab is named for the town of Hele in Wanning, on Hainan's east coast, where the crabs are harvested from the brackish, mineral-rich waters where the Wanquan River meets the South China Sea — an environment long credited with giving the meat its distinctive sweetness. For generations, Hele Crab has been a centerpiece of coastal feasts and festival dinners, and it embodies the Hainanese philosophy that the finest ingredients need little embellishment. During the autumn and winter months, when the crabs are plumpest and their roe richest, it is the island's most sought-after delicacy and a point of deep local pride.

How to Eat:

  1. Crack the claws and legs to extract the sweet white meat
  2. Lift the shell to reveal the golden roe, the prized part
  3. Dip the meat lightly in ginger-vinegar or lime sauce
  4. Use crab tools or your hands — this is a hands-on dish

Price Range: ¥100–200 per crab depending on size and season

Where to Try:

  • Seafood restaurants along Sanya's bays — the classic setting
  • The city's seafood markets and processing stalls — choose a live crab and have it steamed
  • Banquet-style Hainanese restaurants — where it is served as a festive dish

Qingbuliang (清补凉)

Flavor Profile: Qingbuliang is Hainan's beloved cooling dessert — a refreshing bowl of shaved ice, coconut milk, and a colorful medley of toppings that might include red beans, mung beans, barley, taro, sweet potato, sago pearls, peanuts, raisins, and cubes of fresh tropical fruit such as mango, watermelon, and coconut. A chilled coconut-milk base ties everything together, creating a dessert that is at once creamy, lightly sweet, and profoundly refreshing. Some versions are served warm in cooler months, but in Sanya's tropical heat, the icy version is the classic, its many textures and gentle sweetness a perfect antidote to the sun.

Historical & Cultural Story: Qingbuliang has ancient roots: its name, meaning "clear and cool," appears in Chinese texts dating back centuries, where it referred to a medicinal soup believed to dispel heat and restore balance. Over time it evolved from a herbal tonic into the sweet, coconut-based dessert beloved across Hainan today, absorbing Southeast Asian influences along the island's maritime trade routes. For locals, qingbuliang is not just a dessert but a way of life in the tropics — a daily treat and the taste of an island afternoon, sold from countless street stalls and small shops, each with its own secret combination of toppings.

How to Eat:

  1. Stir the coconut milk into the shaved ice and toppings
  2. Scoop up a spoonful with a mix of beans, fruit, and ice
  3. Choose your favorite toppings or ask for "everything"
  4. Enjoy it slowly as the ice melts into the coconut milk

Price Range: ¥10–25 per bowl, depending on the toppings

Where to Try:

  • Street stalls and dessert shops throughout Sanya — the most authentic versions
  • Night markets — a classic end to an evening of eating
  • Beachfront cafés — for a cooler, more leisurely setting

Folk Culture

Li Brocade & Li–Miao Ethnic Culture (黎锦与黎苗文化)

Origin & History: Hainan is home to the Li and Miao ethnic minorities, whose cultures are among the oldest living traditions on the island — the Li are considered Hainan's earliest known inhabitants, with a history stretching back thousands of years. The Li are celebrated above all for their brocade weaving, known as Li brocade or "Lijin," a technique in which women spin, dye, and weave cotton into intricate geometric patterns using a backstrap loom. The craft was inscribed on UNESCO's List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding in 2009, in recognition of both its beauty and its fragility. Each Li community has its own patterns and motifs, which traditionally recorded stories, clan identity, and the rhythms of the natural world — the patterns are a kind of textile language passed from mother to daughter over generations.

Performance/Experience Form:

  • Li Brocade Weaving — watch weavers at work on backstrap looms, creating the intricate patterns
  • The Sanyuesan Festival (三月三) — the Li people's main festival, held on the third day of the third lunar month, with song, dance, and courtship customs
  • Traditional Song & Dance — the Li and Miao perform distinctive songs, bamboo dances, and drumming
  • Ethnic Villages & Textile Museums — settlements and centers where traditional architecture, crafts, and daily life are preserved

Where to Experience:

  • Ethnic cultural parks and heritage villages in the Sanya area — many offer weaving demonstrations
  • The Hainan Provincial Museum in Haikou — for a deeper look at Li brocade and island history
  • During the Sanyuesan Festival — celebrated across Hainan's Li communities each spring

Best Time to Experience: The Sanyuesan Festival in the third lunar month (usually April) is the most vivid time, when Li communities gather for days of song, dance, and celebration; weaving demonstrations are offered year-round at cultural centers

Junpo Festival & the Worship of Madame Xian (军坡节与冼夫人信仰)

Origin & History: The Junpo Festival is one of Hainan's most distinctive folk celebrations, rooted in the worship of Madame Xian (冼夫人), a legendary 6th-century leader of the Li people who is revered across the island and much of southern China. Madame Xian was a brilliant military and political figure who unified the region, defended its people, and was posthumously honored with temples throughout Hainan. The Junpo Festival — also called the "Military Slope Festival" — commemorates her arrival in Hainan and is said to have originated as a day when villages would assemble and parade in her honor, invoking her protection. Held mainly in the first months of the lunar year, it is a time when communities across Hainan come together for parades, opera, and feasting, and it remains one of the island's most important living traditions.

Performance/Experience Form:

  • Parades & Processions — villagers carry statues and banners of Madame Xian through the streets, accompanied by drums and gongs
  • Hainanese Opera (琼剧) — traditional opera performances staged in village squares and temples
  • Temple Worship & Incense — devotees offer incense and prayers at temples dedicated to Madame Xian
  • Feasting & Communal Meals — families and villages share lavish banquets, and the festival is a time of reunion

Where to Experience:

  • Temples dedicated to Madame Xian across Hainan — the focus of the celebrations
  • Town and village squares — where parades and opera performances are held
  • Throughout the island's rural communities — where the festival is most vividly preserved

Best Time to Experience: The Junpo Festival is held in the first to third lunar months (roughly February to April), with the exact dates varying by village; the parades and opera are the highlights to seek out

Travel Tips

Best Time to Visit

High Season (November–April): This is the ideal window for most travelers — dry, sunny, and mild, with daytime highs around 24–28°C and low humidity. The sea is calm and clear, making it the best time for swimming, diving, and beach time. This is also the peak tourist season, so book accommodation and flights well ahead, especially around the Chinese New Year holiday.

Shoulder & Off Season (May–October): Summer is hot, humid, and rainy, with temperatures often above 30°C and the risk of typhoons from roughly July to September. Hotel prices drop sharply, and the island is much quieter, but beach days can be interrupted by storms, and some water sports close during rough weather. If you visit in summer, plan indoor and evening activities and watch the weather forecast.

Avoid:

  • Midday sun exposure without protection — the tropical sun is intense year-round
  • Typhoon season (July–September) if you plan to dive or spend most days at the beach

Recommended Duration

Minimum: 3 full days (Day 1: Yalong Bay + beach; Day 2: Tianya Haijiao + Nanshan; Day 3: Wuzhizhou Island for diving and water sports)

Ideal: 4–5 days — add a leisurely resort day, an evening at a night market, and time to sample the seafood and qingbuliang

Extended: 6–8 days — add day trips to the rainforest, hot springs, and ethnic cultural parks, or combine with a stay in Haikou for the full island experience

Getting Around the City

Taxi & Rideshare: Taxis and DiDi are the most convenient way to get around, especially to the bays and attractions spread along the coast. Fares within the city are reasonable, though longer trips to Wuzhizhou or Nanshan can cost ¥100–150.

Public Bus: An extensive and cheap bus network (¥2–10 depending on distance) connects downtown with Yalong Bay, Tianya Haijiao, Nanshan, and the airport; route numbers are clearly marked but destinations are usually in Chinese only.

Rental & Shuttle: Many hotels offer shuttle services to the main attractions, and renting a car or scooter is popular for exploring the coastal roads at your own pace (an international or Chinese driving license is required for cars).

Intercity Transport

Sanya Phoenix International Airport (SYX): About 15 km northwest of downtown. Airport buses and taxis (about ¥40–50, 20–30 minutes) connect to the city and major resorts. Direct flights link Sanya with Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu, and many other Chinese cities, plus some international destinations.

High-Speed Rail (环岛高铁):

  • Sanya Railway Station is in the city center; the high-speed rail loops the entire island
  • Sanya to Haikou takes about 1.5–2 hours by high-speed train, making island-wide travel easy
  • Trains also connect Sanya with the east-coast towns, useful for reaching Wenchang, Qionghai, and Wanning

Practical Tips

Sun & Weather: The tropical sun is intense — bring high-SPF sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat, and cover up during midday. Even in "winter," sunburn is a real risk. Carry a light rain layer, as tropical showers can arrive suddenly in the warmer months.

Payment:

  • Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted almost everywhere, from hotels to street stalls; link your card before arriving
  • Carry a little cash (¥100–300) for small markets, buses, and rural areas

Swimming Safety:

  • Swim only in designated areas and heed lifeguard flags and warnings
  • Check conditions before water sports, especially in typhoon season

Language:

  • Mandarin is widely spoken, along with the local Hainanese dialect
  • English is limited outside major hotels — download an offline translation app

Etiquette:

  • Dress modestly and be respectful when visiting temples and cultural sites such as Nanshan
  • Do not touch or step on coral when diving or snorkeling — the reefs are fragile and protected
  • Ask before photographing people, especially in ethnic cultural villages and during festivals
