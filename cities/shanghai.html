Core Scenic Spots

Discover the must-visit attractions in 上海

The Bund
★★★★☆ (4.8)
A stunning waterfront promenade showcasing a museum of global architecture from the 1920s and 30s, juxtaposed against the futuristic skyline of Lujiazui across the Huangpu River.
📍 Zhongshan East 1st Road, Huangpu District 🕐 Open 24 hours (Best viewed at dusk and night) 💰 Free (Ferry across the river costs approx. $0.30)

Tips: 1. Arrive by 6:00 AM for a crowd-free sunrise view. 2. Walk south towards the Shanghai Tower for the best photo angles.

✨ Witness the ultimate contrast of colonial-era grandeur and modern Chinese ambition.
History Architecture Nightlife
Yu Garden
★★★★☆ (4.5)
A classical Chinese garden dating back to the Ming Dynasty, featuring exquisite rockeries, zigzag bridges, and tranquil ponds that offer a serene escape from the bustling city.
📍 218 Anren Street, Huangpu District 🕐 08:30 - 17:15 (Closed Mondays) 💰 40 RMB ($5.50) in peak season; 30 RMB ($4) in off-season

Tips: 1. Buy tickets online via WeChat to skip the long queues. 2. Explore the adjacent City God Temple bazaar for local snacks.

✨ Step into a 400-year-old masterpiece of traditional Chinese landscape design.
Garden History Ming Dynasty
Shanghai Tower
★★★★☆ (4.7)
China's tallest building and the world's second-tallest, its twisting, aerodynamic design reduces wind loads while offering breathtaking 360-degree views of the metropolis from its observation deck.
📍 501 Yincheng Middle Road, Pudong New Area 🕐 08:30 - 21:30 (Last entry 20:30) 💰 180 RMB ($25) for the main observation deck

Tips: 1. Book tickets online in advance to secure a time slot. 2. Visit on a clear day; the view is lost in the fog during humid summers.

✨ Ride one of the world's fastest elevators to the clouds for an unmatched skyline view.
Skyscraper Views Modern
Jing'an Temple
★★★★☆ (4.6)
A striking Buddhist temple with golden roofs that contrasts dramatically with the surrounding modern luxury malls, offering a spiritual oasis in the heart of the city's commercial district.
📍 1686 Nanjing West Road, Jing'an District 🕐 07:30 - 17:00 (Daily) 💰 50 RMB ($7)

Tips: 1. Dress modestly and maintain silence in the main halls. 2. Explore the surrounding Jing'an Kerry Centre for high-end dining afterwards.

✨ Experience the surreal harmony of ancient incense smoke against a backdrop of neon and glass.
Temple Buddhism Architecture
Wukang Road (Former French Concession)
★★★★☆ (4.9)
A tree-lined avenue boasting the city's most iconic heritage architecture, including the famous Wukang Mansion, blending European charm with Shanghai's unique historical narrative.
📍 Wukang Road, Xuhui District 🕐 Open 24 hours (Shops/Cafes: 10:00 - 22:00) 💰 Free (Wukang Mansion exterior viewing only)

Tips: 1. Rent a bicycle to cover more ground comfortably. 2. Stop at the Wukang Mansion intersection for the classic Shanghai photo op.

✨ Stroll through the leafy, historic heart of Shanghai's most romantic and trendy neighborhood.

Local Cuisine

Savor the authentic flavors of 上海

Xiao Long Bao (小笼包)
Delicate, translucent dumplings filled with savory pork and a rich, gelatinized broth that bursts into a hot, flavorful soup upon the first bite.
📍 Nanxiang Mantou Dian (Yu Garden) or Jia Jia Tang Bao (Huanghe Road) 💰 $8 - $15 per serving
💡 Dip in black vinegar with ginger shreds, bite a small hole to sip the broth first to avoid burning your mouth.
Sheng Jian Mantou (生煎馒头)
Pan-fried pork buns with a crispy, golden-brown bottom and a soft, fluffy top, sprinkled with sesame seeds and scallions, offering a perfect textural contrast.
📍 Xiao Yang Fry Dumpling (multiple locations) or Dong Tai Po (Shouning Road) 💰 $4 - $8 per order
💡 Eat immediately while hot, beware of the scalding soup inside; pair with a bowl of beef curry soup.
Scallion Oil Noodles (葱油拌面)
A humble yet profoundly flavorful Shanghai staple, consisting of springy wheat noodles tossed in a fragrant, caramelized scallion oil and soy sauce mixture.
📍 Maiden (multiple locations) or local hole-in-the-wall spots in Jing'an 💰 $2 - $5 per bowl
💡 Mix thoroughly before eating, pair with a side of pickled radish or a fried pork chop.
Shanghai Braised Pork Belly (本帮红烧肉)
Melt-in-your-mouth pork belly simmered slowly in a rich, sweet-and-savory sauce of soy sauce, rock sugar, and Shaoxing wine, embodying the essence of Benbang cuisine.
📍 Lao Zheng Xing or Lao饭店 (Old Restaurant) on Fuyou Road 💰 $12 - $20 per portion
💡 Order with steamed white rice to soak up the rich, sweet sauce; it's a heavy dish, so balance it with a green vegetable.
Culture

Culture & Heritage

Experience the cultural soul of 上海

Longtang (Lane House) Life

The traditional residential network of Shanghai, where neighbors share communal spaces, laundry lines crisscross the alleys, and a tight-knit community spirit thrives amidst rapid modernization.

Significance: Represents the communal living, social fabric, and authentic daily rhythm of old Shanghai before the skyline transformed.

Best Time: Early morning or late afternoon when locals are active in the lanes.

How to experience: Wander through the remaining lanes in the outskirts of Xintiandi or the Jing'an district, observe daily life, and chat with locals.

Qipao (Cheongsam) Heritage

The iconic form-fitting dress that became a symbol of Shanghai's 1920s cosmopolitan elegance, blending traditional Chinese tailoring with Western fashion influences.

Significance: Symbolizes the golden age of Shanghai's cosmopolitan culture, female emancipation, and East-meets-West aesthetic in the 1920s-30s.

Best Time: Year-round, but best seen during cultural festivals or at the Shanghai History Museum.

How to experience: Visit a bespoke tailor in the French Concession to get measured, or explore the Qipao exhibition at the Shanghai History Museum.

Travel Guide

Everything you need to plan your trip

Climate

Subtropical monsoon climate with four distinct seasons. Summers (June-August) are hot and humid, while winters (December-February) are damp and chilly. Spring and autumn offer the most pleasant weather.

Recommended Duration

4 to 5 days to explore the core city, including the Bund, French Concession, and museums. Add 2 days for nearby water towns like Zhujiajiao.

Local Transit

The Shanghai Metro is world-class, extensive, and easy to navigate with English signage. The Maglev train connects Pudong Airport to the city outskirts at 430 km/h. Taxis and ride-hailing (DiDi) are convenient.

Getting Here

Shanghai is a major hub. Hongqiao Railway Station offers high-speed trains to Beijing (4.5 hrs), Hangzhou (1 hr), and Nanjing (1.5 hrs). Pudong (PVG) and Hongqiao (SHA) airports serve global and domestic flights.

Travel Tips

Avoid public transport during rush hours (7:30-9:00 AM, 5:30-7:00 PM). Always carry a portable power bank. Download offline maps as some apps may not work perfectly without internet.
Insights

Deep Travel Insights

Honest advice and local knowledge from experienced travelers

Practical Guide

Visa & Entry: Shanghai offers a 144-hour visa-free transit for citizens of 54 countries. For longer stays, apply for a standard tourist visa (L visa) in advance.

Payment: Cash is virtually extinct. Link your foreign credit card (Visa/Mastercard) to Alipay or WeChat Pay before arrival; this is mandatory for almost all transactions.

Connectivity: Buy a local SIM card at the airport or use an eSIM (like Airalo) to bypass the Great Firewall. Free public Wi-Fi is rare and often requires a local phone number to log in.

Language: English is widely spoken in luxury hotels, expat hubs, and major attractions. Download a translation app (like Baidu Translate or Apple Translate) for navigating local markets and taxis.

Honest Tips

The Crowds are Real: The Bund at night is a visual spectacle, but you will be shoulder-to-shoulder with thousands of tourists. For a magical, crowd-free experience, arrive at 6:00 AM to watch the sunrise over Pudong.

Beware the Tea Scam: Near Yu Garden and Nanjing Road, overly friendly locals may invite you to a 'traditional tea ceremony.' This is a notorious scam that will leave you with a bill of hundreds of dollars. Politely decline and walk away.

Tourist Traps vs. Authentic: Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street is a neon-lit commercial jungle that feels more like a generic mall than historic Shanghai. Skip it for the tree-lined streets of the Former French Concession, which offer genuine charm.

Pearl Market Haggling: The AP Plaza (Science and Technology Museum Metro Station) is a maze of fake goods. Vendors will quote you 10x the real price. You must negotiate aggressively (aim for 20-30% of the opening price) or simply walk away.

Local Secrets

Coffee Capital of the World: Shanghai has more coffee shops than any other city globally. Skip the big chains and explore the boutique cafes hidden in the alleys of Jing'an (like Wuding Road) or Xuhui (Yongkang Road) for world-class roasts.

Hidden Art Havens: Instead of the crowded museums, visit M50 Art District in Putuo. It's a former textile mill turned contemporary art gallery complex, offering a gritty, authentic look at Shanghai's underground art scene.

Local Morning Rituals: To see real Shanghai, visit Fuxing Park or Lu Xun Park at 7:00 AM. You'll witness locals practicing Tai Chi, playing chess, and dancing—a vibrant community ritual untouched by the city's skyscrapers.

Secret Viewpoints: Skip the expensive tickets for the Shanghai Tower. Instead, head to the rooftop bar at the Flair on the 58th floor of the Ritz-Carlton Pudong, or simply grab a coffee at the top of the Bund Finance Center for stunning, unobstructed views of the skyline.
