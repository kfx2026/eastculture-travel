Shenyang

A Storied Imperial Capital of the North

Top Attractions

Shenyang Imperial Palace (Mukden Palace)

Location: 171 Shenyang Road, Shenhe District, Shenyang

History: Built in 1625 under Nurhaci and completed by Hong Taiji, this palace served as the early Qing dynasty seat before Beijing. It blends Manchu, Han, and Mongolian architectural styles, witnessing the rise of the Qing Empire.

Highlights:

  • Dazheng Hall (Great Administration Hall) with its unique octagonal pavilion design
  • Phoenix Tower (Fenghuang Lou), the highest point of the old city, offering panoramic views
  • Imperial bedchamber and rare Qing artifacts showcasing Manchu cultural heritage

Hours: April 10 to October 10: 8:30–17:30 (last entry 17:00); October 11 to April 9: 9:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00); closed Mondays (except national holidays)

Tickets: CNY 60 (peak season), CNY 50 (low season); discounted tickets for students and seniors

Transport: Take Metro Line 1 to Huaiyuanmen Station (Exit C), then walk 500 meters; or bus routes 117, 118, 132, 140 to Gongye Bowuguan stop

Duration: 2.5–3 hours

Best season: May to October (cool autumn is especially pleasant)

Tips: Hire an audio guide or join a tour to understand the contrast with Beijing's Forbidden City; combine your visit with nearby Shenyang Zhongjie Pedestrian Street for local snacks.

Mukden Military Academy (Huangpu Military Academy Shenyang Branch)

Location: 1 Huangpu Road, Dadong District, Shenyang

History: Established in 1931 during the Japanese occupation, this academy was rebuilt as a branch of the Whampoa Military Academy in 1947, training officers for the Nationalist army. It now serves as a museum commemorating Northeast China's military history.

Highlights:

  • Preserved classrooms and dormitories of the 1930s
  • Exhibits on the Battle of Shenyang and the defense of the Northeast
  • Historical photos and documents detailing military education in Republican China

Hours: 9:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00), closed on Mondays

Tickets: Free entry (valid ID required)

Transport: Bus routes 132, 259, or 340 to Huangpu Lu stop; about 25 minutes from the city center by taxi

Duration: 1.5 hours

Best season: Year-round (indoor exhibits dominate)

Tips: Combine with the nearby September 18th Historical Museum (Notheastern Army memorial) for a deeper understanding of Shenyang's wartime past. Photography restrictions apply in some halls, so look for signposts.

September 18th Historical Museum

Location: 46 Wanghua South Street, Dadong District, Shenyang

History: Opened in 1999 on the site of the 'Liutiao Lake Incident' on September 18, 1931, this museum memorializes the Japanese invasion of Manchuria. It is a solemn reminder of China's anti-aggression struggle, with a striking 18-meter-tall monument shaped like a calendar.

Highlights:

  • The emblematic 'Sept 18' stone stele and cracked bell sculpture
  • Seven exhibition halls with over 800 photographs and artifacts from the occupation era
  • Interactive displays on the puppet state of Manchukuo and underground resistance movements

Hours: 9:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00), closed on Tuesdays (except national holidays)

Tickets: Free entry (all passport holders must reserve online at least one day in advance)

Transport: Metro Line 4 to Beidaying Station (Exit A), then walk 15 minutes; or bus 227 to Jiuyiba Historical Museum stop

Duration: 2 hours

Best season: Year-round, but more somber and less crowded on weekdays

Tips: Allow extra time on September 18 th when commemorative ceremonies occur. No large bags allowed – use lockers at the entrance. Pair this visit with the Mukden Military Academy for a full day of historical exploration.

Zhaoling Tomb (Beiling Park)

Location: 12 Taishan Road, Huanggu District, Shenyang

History: The tomb of Qing Emperor Huang Taiji (Hong Taiji) and his empress Xiaoduanwen, built in 1651. It is the largest of the three imperial tombs outside the Great Wall, covering 3.3 million square meters, and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties.

Highlights:

  • The majestic Long'en Gate and the sacred way flanked by stone animals
  • The pine–clad park (Beiling Park) with 2,000-year-old trees and a vast lake for boating
  • A winter ice rink when the lake freezes, popular among locals

Hours: Park: 7:00–17:00 (summer), 8:00–16:30 (winter); Tomb interior: 8:00–16:00 (last entry 15:30)

Tickets: Park entrance CNY 20; tomb ticket CNY 50 (includes park access). Discounts for students/seniors.

Transport: Metro Line 2 to Beiling Park Station (Exit B), then 10-minute walk; or bus 228, 245 to Beiling Park East Gate

Duration: 3–4 hours (including park stroll)

Best season: April to June for blossoming trees; October for autumn foliage; winter for ice activities

Tips: Rent a bike or take the electric shuttle inside the park to save time. Climb the City Wall near the tomb's south gate for a bird's-eye view. Visit on a sunny day – the contrast between red walls and northern light is stunning.

Local Cuisine

Lao Bian Dumplings (Lao Bian Jiaozi)

Taste: Thin, chewy wrappers with juicy, umami-rich pork and scallion filling; a savory broth bursts with each bite

Story: Invented in 1872 by Bian Fu and his father, these dumplings became famous as the 'First Dumpling in Shenyang' for their unique preparation – a pork filling with more than 50% soup made from bone broth, frozen and then hand-blended. They were served at the Qing imperial court and later enjoyed by Mao Zedong in the 1950s.

How to eat: Order the classic steamed pork and chive dumplings; dip in black vinegar with shredded ginger. For a full experience, pair with a bowl of clear beef soup.

Price: CNY 40–80 per person

Tieguo Dun (Iron Pot Stew)

Taste: Hearty and deeply savory – chicken, ribs, or fish simmered in a large iron pot with potatoes, corn, and broad beans, flavored with soybean paste and spices; also features hand-pressed corn cakes stuck to the pot's edge

Story: Tieguo Dun originates from the Manchu tradition of communal cooking on a stove, brought to Shenyang by the Qing dynasty's court kitchen. The 'tieguo' (iron pot) is a symbol of rustic Northeastern cuisine, where ingredients are cooked slowly over wood or gas flames, infusing each other's flavors. Today, it is a beloved winter comfort food in Northeastern China.

How to eat: Choose your main meat (perch, chicken, or lamb), then let the pot simmer for 20 minutes. Scrape off the crispy corn cake bits (huāng) from the pot edge before stirring. Enjoy with pickled vegetables and cold beer or baijiu.

Price: CNY 60–120 per person (sharing for 3–4 people)

Shenyang Noodle and Fried Stick (Siji Naizhu)

Taste: A creamy, slightly sweet milk-based noodle soup served with crispy fried dough sticks – a delicate yet indulgent breakfast, with bite-sized noodles in warm, velvety broth

Story: Siji Naizhu (Four Seasons Milk Noodles) originated in Shenyang during the early 20th century, blending Mongolian milk tea tradition with local wheat noodles. It was a favorite of the Qing imperial relatives stationed in the city. The dish was once sold as a midnight snack by street vendors, but now it is a nostalgic breakfast item in Shenyang's old neighborhoods.

How to eat: Order the 'naizhu' (milk noodle soup) with a side of youtiao (fried dough). Sprinkle with sesame and scallions if desired. Best enjoyed hot at a traditional breakfast stall like Siji Naizhu on Jingxing Street. Skip the sugar for a balanced taste.

Price: CNY 15–25 per set

Culture & Traditions

Errenzhuan (Northeastern Folk Opera)

History: Originating in Shenyang and the Liaohe River basin in the late Qing dynasty (around 1850s), Errenzhuan is a duet-format opera blending singing, dancing, and comedic dialogue. It emerged as a grassroots entertainment for farmers and later absorbed elements of Jin Ping Mei and local songs.

Experience: Performances feature two actors (one male, one female) in bright costumes, performing sassy humor, flips, jumps, and rapid-fire storytelling accompanied by stilts, fans, and a unique local percussion ensemble. Some shows include acrobatics and audience interactivity.

Venues: Big Wanxiao Theater (大万巷剧场, 70 Wanxiang Street) or Liu Laogeng Da Wanxiao (刘老根大舞台) – the most famous professional venue in Shenyang, with nightly shows at 7:30 PM.

Best time: Evening performances (around 19:30–21:00) are ideal; shows run year-round, but weekends have a more festive atmosphere.

Significance: Errenzhuan represents the uninhibited, resilient spirit of Northeastern Chinese people, blending humor and social insight in a way that has become a national cultural brand. It also preserves Manchu and Han oral traditions, recognized as a National Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2006.

Tai Chi in the Imperial City Morning Exercise

History: While Tai Chi is common throughout China, Shenyang's early-morning practice sessions at the Fuling Tomb and Zhaoling Tomb date back to the 1920s, when Manchu aristocrats and retired officials gathered to practice martial arts at the imperial tombs. This tradition was later revived in the 1980s, becoming a daily ritual for local residents.

Experience: Groups of 10–50 participants practice Chen-style tai chi and qigong among the ancient pines at dawn, under the guidance of volunteer masters. Visitors can join, observe, or take a private lesson with a local instructor.

Venues: The open-area square in front of Zhaoling Tomb (Beiling Park) and the courtyards adjacent to Fuling Tomb (East Tomb), where locals gather around 6–7 AM, especially on weekends.

Best time: May to September, early morning from 6:30 to 7:30 AM for authentic atmosphere; winter groups are smaller but still exist if weather permits.

Significance: This practice embodies the Taoist concept of harmonizing with nature and the continuity of imperial-era martial arts as community wellness. It is a living connection between Shenyang's historical heritage and its modern urban rhythm, reflecting the city's slow-paced morning culture.

Travel Guide

4.1 Best time to visit

Spring (April to May)： Mild temperatures (10–20°C), blooming lilacs and apricot blossoms in the parks. Perfect for walking around imperial relics without crowd. However, occasional sandstorms from the north – bring a mask.

Autumn (September to November)： Crisp, clear skies, average 10°C to 15°C. The golden leaves of Zhaoling Tomb's pine and poplar groves create the most scenic view. Ideal for photography and outdoor exploration.

Avoid (December to February)： Winter is bitingly cold (-20°C to -5°C) with harsh winds, though the cultural attractions and winter ice sports (lakes at Zhaoling and Fuling) remain open. If you come, dress in extreme layers. Avoid CNY week when tickets are crowded.

4.2 Recommended days

Shortest (1-2 days)： Day 1: Shenyang Imperial Palace and nearby Zhongjie Street for lunch; then September 18th Museum (afternoon). Day 2 (if available): Zhaoling Tomb in the morning, then local hotpot for dinner. Short but packed highlight coverage.

Ideal (3-5 days)： Add a full day to Mukden Military Academy and Qianshan Mountain (or the royal riverfront). Include a night at an Errenzhuan show. Spend a morning sweating with tai chi at Zhaoling. Explore the old industrial area (Tiexi Creative Park) for modern art. Use public transport to ease.

Deep (7+ days)： Combine with nearby cities: taking train Day 1 to Fushun (coal heritage), Day 2–3 to Benxi Water Caves (4 hours away), then back to Shenyang for a deeper dive into Manchuria history (visit Dongbei University and the former Japanese consulate). Also explore local wet markets and cooking classes in the old city. This is a border exploring slow trip.

4.3 Getting around

Metro/Bus： Shenyang Metro has 4 lines (Line 1,2,4,9,10) that reach main landmarks (Imperial Palace, Zhaoling, Museum). Single ride CNY 2–6, rechargeable card (Shenyang Tong) available. Buses cost CNY 1–2 and use coins or mobile QR. For new metro Line 4/10, use Alipay QR.

Taxi / Ride-hailing： Taxis commence at CNY 8, per km ~CNY 1.8 after 3 km. DiDi or CaoCao are widely used. Fares from airport to central Shenyang are about CNY 40–60 (40 mins). Always use apps to avoid overcharging – ask for meter if hiring on street.

Shared bike： HelloBike and Meituan bikes are plentiful within the Second Ring Road. The flat terrain makes biking easy, especially to Beiling Park (4 km from city center). Bikes cost ~CNY 1.5 per 30 min, with monthly passes at CNY 6–10. Beware of winter ice – reduce use from December to February.

4.4 Airport & intercity

Shenyang Taoxian International Airport (SHE)： Located 25 km south of the city. Take Metro Line 2 (airport branch) from Taoxian Airport station to city center (about 1 hour, CNY 7) , or airport shuttle bus 1 to Shenyang North Station (CNY 15). Taxi – CNY 50–70 with luggage. Use east exit if taking metro.

Shenyang Railway Stations： Shenyang North Station (高铁) is the hub for intercity high-speed rail. Metro Line 2 connects it to central Shenyang. For slower trains, Shenyang Yingkou East Station also exists. Use 12306 official app for tickets – book at least 3 days in advance for weekends.

Travel to other cities： High-speed trains approach 350 km/h to Dalian (1.5 hr), Beijing (4 hr), Harbin (2.5 hr). Long-distance sleepers (K-train) go to Manzhouli or Hunchun for border exploration. For quick weekend, Dalian's coastal vibe is the most popular retreat.

4.5 Practical tips

  • Carry valid ID always – free museums, temples and metro security require scanning your passport/Chinese ID.
  • Download a translation app like WeChat or Baidu Translate – English signage outside tourist attractions is still sparse.
  • Expect cold weather later into spring; pack a windproof jacket. Remove shoes when entering Manchu clan palaces (some exhibit areas require it).
  • Book Imperial Palace tickets online via WeChat directly (official 沈阳故宫公众号) – walk-in queues can be 30–60 min, and entry is limited to control crowd.
  • Try local kebabs and 炸串 (fried skewers) at Xing Shun Street night stall, but stay away from roadside stalls selling 'Seafood' – they may not be fresh due to landlocked location.
